# Making money — what's actually realistic, and what I can genuinely do for you

Written August 2026. Figures are UK-focused and are ranges from public
benchmarks, not promises.

## First, the honest bit

I can't make money for you. Not "won't" — can't. Earning money requires
things I don't have and can't have: your identity, a bank account, the legal
ability to sign a contract or accept a payout, and a real human relationship
with whoever is paying. Every route below has at least one step that has to
be you.

There is also no method that is both **free to start** and **effortless** and
**pays well**. Pick two, at most. Anything advertised as all three is either
an investment (which needs capital, not effort) or someone selling you a
course. That's the whole reality check.

What I *can* do essentially all of is **build and ship software**. That's the
one lever where "Claude does the work" is literally true — and it happens to
be the lever you already have set up.

## What you already have (this is worth more than you think)

- An Apple Developer account and an app that has been through App Store
  review (FutureCast — privacy page and support email already live).
- A working Expo/React Native repo with an EAS pipeline: merge to `main`
  auto-publishes an update.
- Me, able to build a complete app in a day or two at no marginal cost.

Most people who want "app income" quit at the boring blockers — the $99/yr
account, provisioning, CI, privacy policy, review rejections. You've already
cleared all of them. Whatever you do next, do it on this foundation rather
than starting something new from zero.

## The options, ranked by how much of the work I can take

### Tier A — I do nearly all the work

**A1. Put a paid tier on an app.**
Add in-app purchases to an existing or new app. I write the code, the
paywall, the receipt handling, the store listing copy. You tap "submit" and
fill in the tax/banking forms Apple requires.

Realistic numbers: iOS free-to-paid conversion runs about **1.4% for weekly
subscriptions, 0.45% monthly, 0.36% annual**. So an app with 1,000 monthly
active users and a £3.99/month tier converts roughly 4–5 people ≈ **£18/month
gross**, £15 after Apple's cut. Apple's [Small Business
Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/) puts
you on 15% commission instead of 30% while you're under $1M/year — apply for
it, it's free money and takes ten minutes.

The honest distribution: most single indie apps earn **£0–50/month, forever**.
A minority reach £100–500/month. The difference is almost never code quality
— it's whether the app solves a recurring, painful problem for an audience
you can actually reach.

Which is the catch: **the binding constraint is distribution, not building.**
I can build ten apps. I cannot make anyone download them. That part is you,
and it's about 30 minutes a week of posting where the relevant people already
are.

**A2. Portfolio, not lottery ticket.**
Because building is nearly free for you now, the right shape is 4–6 small
paid apps over a few months, not one app polished for a year. Expect most to
earn nothing. You're buying tickets cheaply until one hits.

A note on UK Wildfire Watch specifically: it's a genuinely good app, but it's
a poor monetisation target — small UK audience, seasonal, and it's a safety
tool people expect to be free. Keep it free as a portfolio piece. Aim paid
apps at niches where people **already pay for software**: trades and small
business admin, hobbies with expensive gear, professional certifications,
anything where the app saves someone billable time.

**A3. A paid web tool instead of an App Store app.**
Stripe instead of Apple: no 15% cut, no review queue, ship changes in
minutes, and it's linkable — which makes it far easier to market than an app.
Same build effort for me. Usually the better first move.

### Tier B — I do the setup, you do a bit of recurring work

**B4. Sell the skill, not the product.** Building apps fast for other people
— local businesses, one-off tools — is by far the most reliable £/hour here.
It's a job, not passive income, but it pays now and it pays multiples of
everything else on this list. I can do the actual building; you handle the
client conversation and invoicing.

**B5. AI training / data work** (Outlier, Mindrift and similar) pays roughly
**£20–50/hour** and is genuinely available. It's hourly work and I can't do
it for you — they're paying for your judgement, and using AI on it is usually
against their terms and gets accounts banned.

**B6. Digital products** (templates, guides). I can write and design them.
They sell only if someone trusts your name already, so this works *after* one
of the above, not before.

### Tier C — genuinely passive, but money is the input

Investing is the only actually-passive income that exists. It needs capital,
not effort: roughly **£400,000 invested to throw off £20,000/year**. At the
small end, a cash ISA or a low-cost global index fund inside a Stocks &
Shares ISA turns spare cash into a few percent a year with zero ongoing work.
Worth doing with whatever you have, but it won't change your situation
quickly, and I'm not qualified to give you financial advice — that's an
IFA's job.

## Things to walk away from

Anything promising passive income with no capital and no work: dropshipping
courses, "AI automation agency" mentorships, crypto/forex bots, MLM, paid
"faceless YouTube" systems. The tell is always the same — if it reliably
printed money, selling you the method would be a strange way to spend their
time.

## What I'd actually do in your position

Two tracks, because one pays now and one pays later:

1. **Now:** B4 — use the app-building capability as a service for money
   today. Realistic: £500–2,000/month part-time.
2. **Later:** A3 then A1 — I build one small paid web tool aimed at a niche
   that already pays for software. If it gets any traction at all, wrap it as
   an app. Then repeat. You spend ~30 min/week on distribution; I do
   everything else.

Track 2 is the one that can eventually keep paying without you. Track 1 is
what funds your patience while track 2 finds its footing.

## The admin bits only you can do

- **UK trading allowance:** the first **£1,000** of gross side income per tax
  year is tax-free and needs no reporting. Above that you must register for
  Self Assessment. It's £1,000 total across all side income, not per source,
  and it's gross — before costs.
- **Apple payouts** need your bank details, a tax form (W-8BEN), and a
  paid-apps agreement in App Store Connect before a single penny moves.
- **Stripe** needs identity verification and a bank account.

payments wired up, landing page, store listing copy. You'd need to do the
